## (a) Compartments and lifetimes
- Counts: 15 lobe compartments (5 each in α/β, α′/β′, γ) plus pedc (one MBON, one DAN) [A14a]; 21 MBON types = 34 MBONs [A14a, A14b]; 20 DAN types, each to "one, or at most two" compartments [A14a]; ~2000 KCs, 7 types [A14a].
- [A14a] "Convergence of DAN axons on compartmentalized Kenyon cell-MBON synapses creates a highly ordered unit that can support learning". [AR16] abstract: compartments differ in "training requirements for memory formation, decay dynamics, storage capacity and flexibility to learn new associations".
- Protocols: [AR16] single = 60-s odor with 30x1-s light pulses on the DAN; spaced = 10 trainings, 15-min rests. [A12] = 60-s dTrpA1 heat with odor, tested at 2 min, 2 h, 9 h (no 24 h).
| compartment / DAN (line) | sign | after ONE pairing | what makes it long-lasting |
|---|---|---|---|
| γ1+pedc, PPL1-γ1pedc (MB320C; = MB-MP1 in A12) | aversive | minutes to hours: robust, "fully retained after 10 min", "largely decayed by 24 hr" [AR16]; 2 h significant, "decayed rapidly over 9 hours" [A12] | nothing found: no 4-day memory despite the "most robust immediate memory" (fetch says even after 10x spaced) [AR16] |
| γ2+α′1 and α′2+α2, PPL1-γ2α′1 + PPL1-α′2α2 (MB099C; MB-MV1/V1) | aversive | hours: significant immediate [AR16]; 9 h "highly stable" [A12, 5htr1b line] | 10 spaced trainings, 15-min rests: 1-day and 4-day memory [AR16] |
| PPL1 line MB630B (name? fetch gave γ3/α′3) | aversive | weak: immediate "barely detectable" [AR16] | 10 spaced trainings, 15-min rests: 1-day and 4-day memory [AR16] |
| PAM MB-M3 (NP5272; A12 names βsp2, β′a2) | aversive | hours: 2 min significant, 2 h present, 9 h not significant [A12] | not tested |
| α1, PAM-α1 (MB043C) | appetitive | about a day: "slow decay in α1" over 1 d [AR16 Fig 3, 4A] | spaced regime not stated in text read; single-training memory already resists erasure [AR16] |
| γ5 PAM (MB315C, name?) + β′2a PAM (MB109B) | appetitive | immediate plus 1-day "significant appetitive memory" (thin) [AR16] | not stated |

## (b) Promotion or parallel? Sources say parallel; conversion is left open
- [AR16] "our data are consistent with a model in which memory formation and consolidation can occur independently and in parallel in individual MB compartments; this view does not exclude the possibility that network activity facilitates memory consolidation."
- [AR16] "It remains an open question whether short-term memories are converted into long-term memories as biochemical changes in the same synapses or whether these memories are formed separately and in parallel." Abstract: "different learning rules are executed in seemingly parallel memory systems".
- [A12] Intro "it is likely that electric shock induces qualitatively different memory traces in parallel"; Results "each dopamine pathway to the MB establishes a memory component with unique temporal stability"; Discussion traces in segregated synapses "would interact with each other". A12 never discusses STM-to-LTM conversion and stops at 9 h.
- Evidence 1 [A12]: blocking MB-M3 (NP5272) cuts 2-h memory only; blocking MV1/V1 (5htr1b) cuts 9-h only; blocking MP1 cuts every time point. Each pathway owns a slice of the decay curve.
- Evidence 2 [AR16 Fig 4A]: PPL1-γ1pedc + PAM-α1 co-activated with one odor: "strongly aversive" at once, "appetitive after 1 day": "simultaneous formation of an aversive memory in γ1pedc and an appetitive memory in α1, with rapid decay ... in γ1pedc and slow decay in α1".
- Evidence 3 [AR16]: the strongest immediate memory (γ1pedc) gives no 4-day memory; "barely detectable" immediate memory (MB630B) gives 4-day memory after spacing. Fast-trace strength does not predict slow-trace memory.
- Evidence 4 [AR16]: stabilisation happens in place: 1-day memory from spaced training with MB099C or MB630B "does not show a significant reduction" after DAN activation, unlike single-training memory. In α1, MBON-α1 "appears to form synapses on the dendrites" of PAM-α1, "a recurrent circuit necessary for long-term memory formation".
- Traces still interact [A12]: two pathways together give "not a simple sum", non-linear and retention-time specific (MV1 with M3 at 2 h).

## (c) Write-rule timing
- [H19] abstract only, verbatim: associations "can be written and reversed on a trial-by-trial basis depending on the temporal relationship between an odor cue and dopaminergic reinforcement"; "bidirectional neural plasticity in the mushroom body"; "DopR1 and DopR2 ... coupling to distinct second messengers and directing either synaptic depression or potentiation". No forward/backward, seconds or cAMP in the abstract: UNVERIFIED.
- My recollection, NOT read (treat as unverified): forward (odor then DAN) = depression via DopR1 (dDA1); backward (DAN then odor) = potentiation via DopR2 (DAMB).
- Read instead [AR16], PPL1-γ1pedc: DAN stimulation inside "a 30-s time window following the onset of a 10-s odor presentation" = aversive; DAN stimulation that "precedes odor presentation by 20 to 60 s" = appetitive. MB099C aversive only at ISI 0 s; MB043C, MB213B, MB315C+MB109B appetitive at ISI +10 and +30 s; optimum "similar, but not identical" across compartments.
- [AR16] "a single DAN can bi-directionally alter the strength of KC-MBON synapses depending on the presence and relative timing of odor-driven KC activity"; DAN with odor lowers "the MBON's attractive output"; DAN alone minutes after training "can almost completely abolish the conditioned response".

## (d) Active forgetting
- [B12] abstract only, verbatim: "the bidirectional modulation of a small subset of dopamine neurons (DANs) after olfactory learning regulates the rate of forgetting of both punishing (aversive) and rewarding (appetitive) memories"; "Two of these DANs, MP1 and MV1, exhibit synchronized ongoing activity in the mushroom body neuropil ... before and after learning"; dDA1 "essential for the acquisition of memory", DAMB "is required for forgetting". No decay times or numbers in the abstract: timescale and with/without-signal numbers UNVERIFIED.
- Same cells as the writers? At cell-type level yes: MP1 and MV1 are among the pathways whose activation writes aversive memory [A12, AR16]; [B12] "dual role for dopamine: memory acquisition through dDA1 signaling and forgetting through DAMB signaling in the mushroom body neurons". Write versus erase is set by receptor [B12] and by whether KCs are active [AR16]. (My recollection, not read: DopR2 of H19 = DAMB.)
- Corroboration [AR16]: cites B12 ("Dopamine signaling to KCs has been implicated in both learning and forgetting"); a second DAN activation without odor a few minutes after training reduced immediate memory in 4 of 5 DAN sets, PAM-α1 resistant; spontaneous decay over about 1 d differs by compartment (fast γ1pedc, slow α1). [A12] MP1 result: "dopamine may play opposing roles in the formation and the degradation of ARM".

## (e) Readout
- Signed sum [A14b]: "co-activation of MBON cell types with opposing effects resulted in intermediate responses"; same-sign pairs give "a stronger response than to either driver alone"; "consistent with a simple combinatorial model of valence integration"; "the MBON ensemble collectively represents valence"; authors propose that local dopaminergic modulation "selectively alters the balance within the MBON network" for the trained stimulus. Net sign flipping over time is also seen in [AR16] Fig 4A.
- Transmitter map [A14b]: "all MBONs eliciting aversion were glutamatergic and all the MBONs eliciting attraction were either GABAergic or cholinergic".
- Untrained-state bias: UNVERIFIED. The A14b text I could read has no statement; the abstract's "alters the balance" implies a baseline balance (my reading).
- MBON-to-MBON: only 3 MBON types send axons into the lobes (GABAergic MBON-γ1pedc>α/β, glutamatergic MBON-γ4>γ1γ2, third name garbled): "three MBON types form a feedforward network in the lobes" [A14a], "a multilayered feed-forward network" [A14b]; MBON-γ1pedc>α/β has "potential to directly influence the activity of additional MBON cell types"; "several other MBONs appear to target the dendrites of specific DANs" [A14b]. Behavioural effect of these links not shown in the text read; known case is MBON-α1 to PAM-α1 [AR16].

## (f) Capacity and interference
- [AR16 Fig 3F] two odor pairs (A/B pentyl acetate/3-octanol; C/D ethyl lactate/4-methylcyclohexanol) trained in sequence: PPL1-γ1pedc line kept "only the most recently trained odor", "an effective memory capacity of one"; PAM-α1 line kept both, "at least two" (no larger number tested).
- [AR16] update strategies: "(1) writing a new memory, while diminishing the old memory; or (2) writing a new memory, while retaining the old memory"; α1 shows "the least ability to replace an older association"; its capacity "is not due to generalization, since training with one odor pair did not affect the innate odor preference ... with a different, untrained odor pair".
- Not found in the text read: any count of odors per compartment or per MB, or interference between similar odors (the tested odors are chemically distinct). [A14a] says only "Odors activate sparse subpopulations of KCs distributed across the MB without spatial preference".

## Inference (mine)
- The sources support parallel tiers on one sparse code, not promotion: no fast-to-slow transfer is shown, conversion is called "open" [AR16], and fast-trace strength does not predict slow-trace memory.
- What is fly-like is repetition-gated stabilisation inside the slow tier (10 spaced trainings, then erasure-resistant): a repeat count gating direct writes to the slow table, not a copy out of the fast one.
- Tiers differ in write rules (timing, sign, overwrite versus retain, capacity 1 versus >=2, erasability), not only decay rate; readout is a signed sum yet traces interact non-linearly [A12]; forgetting is DAN-driven [B12], so event-tied decay may fit better than a clock.
- Caveat: lobes read different KC subpopulations (γ versus α′/β′ versus α/β; background knowledge, not read), so "same key" is an approximation.
