# fly-read-1-key: what six primary sources constrain about a KC-style (similarity-preserving) table key (2026-10-09)
All six opened. WebFetch summaries mis-stated some items, so every number below was re-read in primary text (PMC/bioRxiv HTML; BioHash arXiv PDF saved by the tool). Tags: [FH17] Dasgupta-Stevens-Navlakha bioRxiv v1 main text (its SI and the Science version NOT opened); [DS18] PNAS 2018; [LK17] Litwin-Kumar 2017; [H15] Hige 2015; [L14] Lin 2014; [BH20] Ryali et al. (arXiv v2/ICML). "mine" = my arithmetic or inference.
## (a) FlyHash construction and what it preserves
- [FH17] Fly: d=50 PN types -> m=2000 KCs (40-fold). Each KC sums ~6 randomly chosen PNs: sparse BINARY matrix, entry probability p=6/d, no weights. APL feedback keeps only the highest ~5% of KCs (100 of 2000 per [DS18]); winners keep their values; the tag is those k values. [BH20] intro: ~2500 KCs, ~50% of PNs active per odour (input is dense).
- [FH17] Normalisation: the PN stage "centers the mean" by divisive normalisation (rates ~exponential with the same mean for every odour and concentration) "so the fly does not mix up odor intensity with odor type". What the benchmarks did to their inputs: not in the main text.
- [FH17] Preserved: l2 (Euclidean) neighbourhoods. Lemma 1 covers only steps 1-2: l2 distance kept in expectation, ||y|| concentrated when m=O(d); the top-k step is justified empirically. Adopts the LSH definition Pr[h(p)=h(q)]=sim(p,q) but shows no collision-vs-distance curve in the main text. Unrelated odour tags "share few, if any, active neurons".
- [FH17] Benchmarks: SIFT d=128, GLOVE d=300, MNIST d=784; 10,000 items; truth = top 2% (200) Euclidean neighbours; mAP over 50 trials; hash length k = number of nonzeros in the tag (LSH: k projections).
- [FH17] Settings: final fly m=10d with 10% of inputs per KC ("most consistent" vs 1%; "no improvement" at 50%); ablation used m=20k (compute-matched to LSH). No sweep of m and no sweep of k/m as a fraction is in the text; the fly's 5% is never tested as a fraction.
- [FH17] Effects: sparse binary ~ dense Gaussian projection (near-identical mAP, 20x cheaper); top-k vs k random KCs: SIFT k=4 32.4% vs 17.7% mAP; full fly vs LSH: MNIST k=4 44.8% vs 16.0% ("almost 3x", "highest for very short codes"); winner indices as a discrete code beat binarising each KC at 0 (Fig S3, no number in text).
- [BH20] Tab.1 (MNIST, class-label truth), FlyHash vs LSH at k=2/8/32/64: 18.94 vs 12.45, 24.24 vs 18.07, 32.30 vs 26.20, 38.41 vs 32.30: ratio falls 1.52 -> 1.19 (mine). BH20 calls small k in {2,4,8,16,32} the regime where FlyHash beat LSH in [FH17].
## (b) Generalisation across stimuli vs KC-code overlap
- [H15] "33% of MCH-responding KCs and 30% of OCT-responding KCs respond to both" odours. Pairing OCT with dopamine-neuron activation: paired MBON spikes 118+-8.3 -> 24+-7.4 (n=7; -80+-5.7%; EPSC charge -90+-3.7%); unpaired MCH 110+-11 -> 83+-14, i.e. -27+-7.1% (4-pulse; -20+-6.3% with 1 pulse); reciprocal pairing "equivalent". Spill-over/paired = 0.34 vs overlap 0.30-0.33 (mine; authors do not compare; spikes are not weights).
- [H15] Four odours (overlap from Campbell 2013; percentages not in the text): pairing PA depresses PA, BA, HP (p<0.005; BA/HP "only marginally less than PA") but not EL (p>0.1); pairing EL depresses EL (p<0.01), HP (p<0.05), others n.s. Depression vs overlap: Pearson r=0.90, p<0.005, grey linear fit (Fig 7I). No threshold or other form is stated; with 4 odours linear vs threshold is unresolvable (mine). Flies generalise PA avoidance to BA/HP (n=10, p<0.01).
- [H15] Write rule: only KC->MBON synapses active around dopamine depress (heterosynaptic; no MBON spiking needed; KC responses unchanged), so spill-over happens only via shared KCs. Authors frame sparseness as what limits it ("a practical limit to how effectively sparse coding can relieve synaptic interference even with such different odors").
- [DS18] Same idea as a data structure: m slots (KC->MBON weights); insert = reset the item's k active slots; query = MEAN of the k active slots' weights, so response falls with the fraction of shared slots: recorded novelty vs % shared KCs r=-0.94 (k=20 of m=350, ~5%); vs ORN Euclidean distance r=0.91.
- [DS18] Fly filter vs distance-sensitive Bloom filter (corr. with ground-truth novelty): odours n=110 0.657 vs 0.537; faces n=2,000 0.508 vs 0.404; SIFT n=5,000 0.535 vs 0.345; classic Bloom ~0.00. Table m=30n (m=10n "similar"), k in [5,50]; SI unopened.
## (c) APL and sparseness
- [L14] Normal: "only ~5-10% of Kenyon cells respond to any given odor" (introduction, cites earlier work); PN responses are broad. Blocking APL (shits1) or KC output (shits1/TeTx): KC responses "much broader and more similar"; population sparseness down, inter-odour correlation up (Fig 5c-e); values are in figures only, not text: unverified.
- [L14] Behaviour: similar pair IA:EB 1:4 vs 4:1 impaired with APL blocked at 32 C (interaction P=0.0012; 3-way with pair similarity P=0.009); dissimilar delta-DL vs IA:EB 4:1 (P=0.53) and OCT vs MCH unaffected. Input correlations: IA vs EB ORN r=0.47, PN r=0.55; delta-DL vs IA/EB ORN r=0.05/-0.07, PN r=-0.05/-0.04; modelled PN population sparseness 0.50 (delta-DL), 0.42 (IA), 0.35 (EB).
- [L14] "Sparse coding is important only insofar as it decorrelates odor representations: even if the sparseness of the CS+ representation is reduced, learned discrimination is unaffected if the correlation between the CS+ and CS- representations remains low." Partial APL loss (GAD RNAi): sparseness unchanged, KC output modestly up ("can improve learning" in earlier studies); a large rise "overwhelms" separate coding of similar odours.
- [L14] Mechanism: KC->APL->KC global feedback; "only adding global inhibition provides the modeled Kenyon cells with the flexibility to respond sparsely to a wide range of odors"; thresholded summation alone sits "in a narrow, unstable band between silence and indiscriminate firing".
## (d) Litwin-Kumar 2017: small in-degree, frozen vs learned
- [LK17] Distinct-wiring ("birthday") argument: K* = smallest in-degree for which all M units get different K-subsets of N inputs with probability >=95% of max: N=50, M=2000 -> K*=7 (p=0.88/0.98/0.996 at K=6/7/8; observed mean 7); cerebellum N=7000, M=209,000 -> K*=4. The 7 and 4 come from this criterion; the dimension-based optimum (next bullets) is a separate calculation that lands near them.
- [LK17] Dimension: input currents dim(h) ~ N/(1+N/M+(K-1)^2/N) (Eq.2; fraction reconstructed by me from garbled HTML); units share K^2/N inputs (mean correlation K/N), so K>1 lowers dim(h). Thresholded layer, f=0.1, N=1000: dim(m) peaks at K=9 (K=19 with log-normal weights); with tuned global inhibition no peak, 95% of max at K=29 (~3% connectivity).
- [LK17] Fixed synapse budget S=MK: f=0.1, S=14,000 -> K=4 (no inhibition) / 8 (inhibition); f=0.01, S=8.4e5 -> K=4; "do not depend strongly" on f. Expansion: dim(m) ~ 1/(1/M+<rho>^2+Var(rho)) saturates; at f=0.1 "expansion ratios beyond 10-50 do not increase the mixed-layer dimension" (fly 40x inside); lower f raises dimension but cuts readout SNR.
- [LK17] Frozen vs learned: "small synaptic degree is sufficient to obtain optimal or near-optimal classification performance when the connections and synaptic weights of the mixed-layer neurons are set randomly and the activities of input-layer and mixed-layer neurons are normalized". Learned input weights help only at K>10 and "may not improve performance" at small K with normalised inputs; learned codes have lower dimension on arbitrary new patterns; unsupervised learning matters mainly when inputs are NOT normalised.
- [LK17] Setup behind that: Hebbian plastic readout on a frozen expansion, tested on noise-corrupted copies of P=1000 stored patterns (Gaussian noise 0.3x signal SD, or 10% bit flips of binary inputs). The paper never says frozen "beats" learned, only that supervised learning of input weights did not help at small K with normalised inputs. For discrete inputs sparse wiring gave LOWER noise sensitivity than dense ("except for very small K": K=1-3 not shown, no threshold gives f in (0,0.1]).
- [LK17] Global inhibition has two jobs: normalisation (one graded unit suffices; handles inputs of differing coding level, which a fixed threshold cannot) and decorrelation (needs tuned weights or many units; helps only at large K).
## (e) BioHash (learned Hebbian expansion) vs FlyHash (random)
- [BH20] BioHash: Krotov-Hopfield local rule (p=2, dense W, m x d), top-k winners, m=k/activity; FlyHash baseline m=10d, 10% sampling. MNIST mAP@All, class-label truth, k=2/4/8/16/32: FlyHash 18.94/20.02/24.24/26.29/32.30 -> BioHash 44.38/49.32/53.42/54.92/55.48 (saturates ~k=16). CIFAR-10 mAP@1000: 14.62/16.48/18.01/19.32/21.40 -> 20.47/21.61/22.61/23.35/24.02.
- [BH20] FlyHash's own protocol (GloVe d=300, neighbour truth, mAP@100, activity 1%), k=2/8/32/64: Euclid FlyHash 13.95/21.15/39.72/54.24 -> BioHash 23.06/43.21/56.94/62.87; cosine 15.06/24.64/50.96/72.37 -> 38.13/66.85/84.05/89.78. Relative gain shrinks as k grows.
- [BH20] Ablation NaiveBioHash (same learning, no expansion): MNIST 25.85/29.83/28.18/31.69/36.48 (beats FlyHash at every k, by 0.1-9.8 points, but far below BioHash); CIFAR-10 11.79-18.65 ~ LSH. Optimal activity is data-dependent: 5% MNIST, 0.25% CIFAR-10 (0.5% used), 1% GloVe; m=k/a is 320 at MNIST k=16 vs FlyHash 7,840 (~25x fewer units, mine).
- [BH20] Tab.3 (MNIST, BioHash vs LSH, k=2..32): input-vs-code similarity correlation, nearest 10% of pairs 57.5-81.3% vs 20.1-62.4%, farthest 10% 0.8-3.1% vs 4.6-19.4%: local similarity kept, global dropped. Only collision law given: uniform circle, k=1, P(different code)=m*theta/2pi up to theta=2pi/m, then 1 (inequality misprinted in the PDF; my reading).
- [BH20] Cost: unlabelled training pass (<20 epochs; MNIST ~1.7 s at k<=8, ~5 s at k=32; CIFAR-10 on VGG16 features 4.2-35 s; one V100), data-mean centering, cross-validated activity, dense weights; FlyHash is data-independent and sparse-binary. Both store k*log2(m) bits/item. Authors: "difficult to provide theoretical guarantees"; main tables use class-label truth. Suggested, untested: f(x) ~ sum over the k winners of gamma_mu, i.e. a per-slot value table.

## Inference (mine)
- Key = frozen sparse-binary expansion (~10% of inputs per slot, m >= 10d) of a normalised context vector, fixed top-k winners (global competition, not a threshold), table address = winner indices, readout = sum/mean of the k slot values ([FH17], [LK17], [L14], [DS18]).
- "Near" is set by the input encoding, since only l2 on that vector is preserved: a positional one-hot gives Hamming-style closeness (substituted bytes), not shifts or insertions, and is sparse where the sources' inputs are dense; no source covers discrete sequences.
- k, m/n and slots-per-item do not transfer: fly 5%; BH20 optima 0.25-5%; FH17 gains largest at k<=8; DS18 used m=10-30n with k=5-50. Sweep them on held-out bits/byte.
- Expect interference along with transfer (30-33% shared KCs leaked 27%); keep an exact-hash table as control and measure overlap between contexts that need different next bytes ([L14]).
- A learned BioHash-style expansion is a later arm (training pass, dense weights, tuned activity); [LK17] says frozen random is near-optimal at small K with normalised inputs.
