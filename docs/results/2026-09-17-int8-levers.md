# Int8-master levers: annealing the sign step recovers 44% of the QAT gap; a live row scale reverses at 30k — 2026-09-17

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-16-int8-levers-design.md`
**Driver:** `runs/int8-levers-2026-09-16/run_levers.py` (screen and 30k stages) and
`run_bits_matched.py` (matched-step bit-width follow-up); results in `screen-results.json`,
`results-30k.json`, `fullval/results.json`
**Status:** 25M text8, 15-code references, three levers, 5k screen plus 30k on three seeds.
Verdict: **decay is real and large** (−0.018 against the constant step on every seed, the spec's
−0.01 line); live scale and fewer bits are rejected.

The master-weight-free penalty at one byte per weight is now **0.044 nats**, down from the
ratchet's 0.078: a stateless integer update with no optimizer state and no floating master
recovers **44% ± 2%** of the gap the repository has been carrying since June. The advance is
spread over every match-length bin (decay − constant: −0.016 on bin 0, −0.020 on bin 1, −0.018 on
2 to 3, −0.011 on 4 to 7, −0.010 on copyable 8+ bytes), like the gap itself.

## Lever 1: annealing the sign step (`--int8-lr-final`, kept)

The grid-unit step falls linearly from `int8_lr` to `int8_lr_final` over the run. Stochastic
rounding already realizes fractional steps, so no new state and no new mechanism.

| Cell | Best at 5k | Best at 30k, seed 1337 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| constant 1.0 (reference) | 1.1802 | 1.0440 |
| → 0.5 | 1.1771 | — |
| → 0.25 | 1.1770 | **1.0253** |
| → 0.1 | 1.1790 | — |

At 5k every decay cell sits within 0.003 of the constant step, inside the 0.005 noise bar: the
screen says "no advance". At 30k the same setting is 0.019 ahead, six times the screened effect,
and the lead grows monotonically (0.003 at 5k, 0.011 at 20k, 0.019 at 30k). **This is the second
time a 5k screen has mis-ranked this family** (the int8 arm itself only passes the ratchet near
step 12k). Screens are for rejecting mechanisms that are broken, not for ranking schedules.

All three decay runs reach their best at step 30,000 exactly, while the constant-step runs peak
near 29,200 and drift up. The recipe is still improving at the budget's end, so 1.0275 is an upper
bound on its converged value.

## Lever 2: live row scale (`--int8-live-scale`, rejected)

A row doubles its scale when more than 1% of its weights sit at the rail (halving the integers with
stochastic rounding) and halves it when its largest weight falls below a quarter of the range. One
extra FP32 per row (+147,564 bytes, 0.6%).

| Arm, seed 1337 | Best at 5k | Best at 30k |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| frozen scale, constant step | 1.1802 | 1.0440 |
| live scale | **1.1729** | 1.0772 |
| live scale + decay to 0.25 | — | 1.0731 |

Live scale is the only cell that *clears* the 5k bar (−0.007, and −0.036 at step 1k) and it ends
30k **0.033 worse than the frozen scale and 0.015 worse than the plain ratchet**. The checkpoints
say why: the median row scale ends at 8× its init value (max 32×) and the trained weights' RMS is
4.8× the frozen run's. Growth is one-way in practice — a row doubles when it touches the rail, but
the shrink condition (max |w| ≤ max/4) cannot fire after a doubling — so the scale ratchets
outward and the effective weights random-walk with it. **The frozen rail was doing useful work: it
is the only thing bounding the weight norm under a sign step.** Decay slows the drift (scales still
reach 8×) and recovers only 0.004 of the damage.

This retires the "the frozen scale is the shared handicap" hypothesis in the form that motivated
it. The init-scale screen showed a *larger frozen* scale is worse; this shows a *growing* scale is
worse; the ratchet's and int8's frozen ±row_max box is not the thing holding either arm back.

## Lever 3: fewer bits (`--int8-bits`, rejected at this budget)

The first pass kept the step at 1.0 grid units, which at 6 and 4 bits is a 4× and 18× larger step
in *effective* units — a step-size test, not a bit-width test (the init-scale screen had already
shown that a 4× step is ruinous). Those cells are kept as such. The follow-up holds the effective
step equal to the 8-bit run (`lr = max_value / 127`).

| Cell at 5k | Best | Saturated | Move % | Bytes |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 8 bits, lr 1.0 (reference) | 1.1802 | 1.17% | 99.5 | 25,327,212 |
| 6 bits, lr 1.0 (step 4× too large) | 1.3778 | 5.47% | 97.8 | 19,032,300 |
| 4 bits, lr 1.0 (step 18× too large) | 2.5382 | — | — | 12,737,388 |
| 6 bits, lr 0.244 (matched step) | 1.2588 | 3.71% | 24.0 | 19,032,300 |
| 4 bits, lr 0.055 (matched step) | 1.5099 | 13.78% | 5.1 | 12,737,388 |

Matching the drift does not rescue the coarse grids: 6 bits is 0.079 behind 8 bits and 4 bits is
0.33 behind. Stochastic rounding delivers the right *average* step but not the right *noise* — a
6-bit weight moves a whole grid unit 24% of the time and each move is 4× larger in effective units,
so the per-step variance is about 4× the 8-bit arm's, and saturation triples. The byte is not
merely storage for this update rule; its resolution sets the noise floor. Fewer bits would need a
different update rule, not a smaller lr.

## What follows

- Anneal further and longer: the winner had not converged at 30k, and 0.25 was the middle of three
  screened endpoints (0.1 was worse *at 5k*, where the screen has now twice been wrong). A 60k run
  and endpoints below 0.25 are the obvious next cells.
- The remaining 0.044 to QAT is Adam's per-weight state plus the live-scale-with-a-bound that QAT
  gets for free. A bounded live scale (grow only while the row's weight norm is below a target) is
  the one live-scale variant this study does not reject.
- Re-run the gap-versus-scale ladder's 99M rung with the decay recipe before repeating the
  "gap grows with scale" claim: that claim is about the plain ratchet.

## Limits

One model size and corpus (25M text8, 15-code references), one seed for every 5k screen cell and
for the live/combined 30k arms, three seeds only for the winner. The schedule is defined as a
fraction of the step budget, so a different budget anneals at a different rate; nothing here
transfers to a run of another length without re-screening. Bit-width cells are 5k only. Runs are
not bit-repeatable (`docs/results/2026-09-15-repeatability.md`); the 30k three-seed SDs above are
the honest spread. No speed or memory claim beyond the counted bytes.
