## (a) Does the trace move?
- A dependency shift from three mushroom-body (MB) neuron classes to one: early memory needs α'/β' and γ neurons, then retrieval becomes independent of them while LTM depends wholly on α/β [CS13 abstract]. Method: shibire-ts block at 32 C for 10 min before and during the test, trained at 24 C, six times from 15 min to 24 h [CS13].
- Appetitive, retrieval-time block: α'/β' abolishes memory at 15 min-3 h (p ≤ 0.0022), no effect at 9-24 h (p ≥ 0.2403); γ cuts ~30-50% at 15 min-3 h, none at 9-24 h (p ≥ 0.3315); α/β cuts ~30% of STM/ITM and abolishes 24 h LTM (p ≤ 0.045); α/β+γ (247-gal4) abolish memory at every time 15 min-24 h [CS13].
- Aversive: α'/β' block hits 15 min-1.5 h (p ≤ 0.0043) but not 3 h or spaced-training 24 h LTM (p ≥ 0.4452); γ hits 15 min-3 h (p ≤ 0.0260), not 24 h (p = 0.4286); α/β hits 15 min-24 h (p ≤ 0.0095). A second odor pair repeats it: α'/β' and γ block hit 1 h not 24 h (p = 0.0022 vs 0.3776; 0.0135 vs 0.6304) [CS13].
- Imaging (G-CaMP3 CS+/CS- ratio, n = 10-16): α'/β' enhanced at 1 h not 24 h (appetitive p = 0.0041 vs 0.8361; aversive p = 0.0011 vs 0.2618); α/β enhanced at 24 h not 1 h (appetitive p = 0.0183 vs 1; aversive p = 0.0027 vs 0.2225) [CS13]. α/β response starts ~9 h after aversive spaced training (Yu 2006, cited in CS13, unopened).
- Chronic silencing (Kir2.1 + TARGET) 6 h before a second training with another odor pair: α'/β' silenced abolishes the new memory (p ≤ 0.0022) and leaves the old one intact (p = 0.6298); α/β silenced impairs both, incompletely (new p = 0.0159, old p = 0.0129) [CS13].
- Silencing right after training: [P17] MP1 dopamine neurons blocked 3 h at 33 C straight after spaced training give a 24 h LTM defect (p = 0.0011 and 0.023 for two drivers), massed memory spared. Via citations in [CS13], originals unverified: α'/β' and DPM output are needed in the consolidation window (Yu 2006; Krashes 2007; Krashes and Waddell 2008); an α/β block 6 h after training impairs LTM (Huang 2012).
- Authors' model [CS13 Fig 5F]: "Initial encoding occurs in all three subsets"; consolidation "reinforces the α/β memory trace" while α'/β' and γ traces "decay, perhaps partly through dopamine-dependent forgetting". An α'/β'-to-α/β interaction that "guides" LTM is their inference, untested. Fly STM is protein-synthesis independent, unlike mammalian recent memory [CS13].
- Beyond the MB: only a citation of Chen 2012 ("other neuron types") in [CS13], unread. [DC13] abstract only: "biochemical consolidation" is signalling inside a neuron, "systems consolidation" is "ongoing communication between brain regions or cell types".

## (b) What the long-lasting form needs
- Spacing, not repetition: 1 cycle (1 min CS+ with 12 x 5 s 60 V shocks, 45 s, 1 min CS-) gives nothing long-lasting; 5 massed cycles give 24 h ARM (no protein synthesis); 5 cycles with 15 min ITI give protein-synthesis-dependent LTM ("normally requires at least five spaced cycles"); 2 spaced cycles do not [P17 Methods, Fig 1a, Results].
- Protein synthesis: LTM is cycloheximide-sensitive (35 mM CXM fed 14-18 h pre-training) [P17]. Single-session aversive memory is protein-synthesis independent; appetitive single-session memory lasts days and becomes protein-synthesis dependent by 6 h [CS13, citing Krashes and Waddell 2008; Colomb 2009].
- Energy gate [P17]: what rises is mitochondrial pyruvate flux in MB neurons (Pyronic + azide, 30 min-2.5 h after training; slope higher after spaced than unpaired or massed, F2,121 = 15.37, p < 0.0001, n = 22/26/16), not glycolysis (p = 0.32, 0.67), not ellipsoid body or neurosecretory cells; whole-fly sucrose intake doubles in the first 4 h after spaced training (p = 0.007), not 4-24 h (p = 0.51).
- Quote: "upregulation of mushroom body energy flux is both necessary and sufficient to drive long-term memory formation" [P17 abstract]. Necessary: PDHE1β RNAi (p = 0.028) and PDP RNAi (p = 0.027) impair 24 h memory after 5 spaced cycles, not massed. Sufficient: PDK RNAi gives CXM-sensitive 24 h memory after only 2 spaced cycles (p = 0.012; CXM p = 0.007) but adds nothing after 5: a gate, "once the gate is open ... LTM is formed at similar magnitude".
- Trigger: MP1 dopamine neurons via the D5-like DAMB receptor; dTrpA1 activation of MP1 during 2 spaced cycles lifts 24 h memory (p = 0.0006) [P17]. Cost: starvation before and after training blocks LTM; LTM-trained flies deprived of food and water die prematurely (refs in P17 Discussion).
- Sleep [B15]: sleep after learning blocks dopamine-neuron (DAN) forgetting; ongoing MV1/MP1/PR DAN activity (TH-GAL4, c150-GAL4) rises with locomotion and falls with sleep. Gaboxadol (0.1 mg/ml, 1 h after learning) raises retention at 6 h (p ≤ 0.005, n = 16) and 8 h (p ≤ 0.015, n = 12); dFB sleep-circuit (R23E10) stimulation raises 3 and 6 h retention (p ≤ 0.0213, n = 8). Adding c150-DAN stimulation to induced sleep leaves memory low (p < 0.0009), so forgetting sits downstream of sleep. Sleep-specific consolidation is "not eliminated"; reduced forgetting may be its prerequisite ("opportunistic consolidation") [B15].

## (c) Forgetting
- Default decay of a one-cycle aversive memory: "near zero by 24 h" [CS13]; PI roughly 0.74 immediately, 0.49 at 3 h, 0.17-0.20 at 6 h, 0.14-0.17 at 8 h, eyeballed from B15 Figs 6E, 6D, 4C, 4D (different cohorts, +-0.02).
- The forgetting signal is state-gated: it "does not chronically remove memories at a constant rate" [B15]. 80 min of arousal (2 s shake per min) right after learning cuts 3 h memory ~0.49 to ~0.25 (p < 0.0013, n = 8; eyeballed) but not acquisition; blocking c150-DAN output prevents the loss [B15].
- Does consolidation protect against it? Not tested: every [B15] retention test is ≤ 8 h after one cycle. [CS13] only models "dopaminergic neurons carry out active forgetting from all sets of neurons". MP1 sits on both sides, in the forgetting DAN set [B15] and as the LTM gate [P17]; no source reconciles them.

## (d) Complementary learning systems
- Why two systems [K16]: a parametric cortical learner must be slow (each experience is one sample, so a small learning rate aggregates the statistics; weights are interdependent and early error signals "noisy and weak"). Alone it cannot act on one experience (lion at the waterhole) and fast weight change causes "catastrophic interference". The hippocampus stores items fast with sparse codes (DG 0.5-1%, CA3 2.5%) but alone has limited capacity and generalization.
- Replay [K16]: hippocampus replays new experience to neocortex "interleaved with replay and/or ongoing exposure to other experiences", so it joins "the database of experiences that govern the values of the connections"; offline (sleep, rest), time-compressed ~10x; causal test: optogenetic block of CA3 output after fear learning cuts CA1 ripples and impairs consolidation. Which memories to interleave is "an open question"; replay is reweighted to surprising or rewarding events so the cortex is "not a slave to the statistics of its environment".
- Revision [K16]: cortical learning depends on prior knowledge "rather than slow per se": schema-consistent items were learned rapidly without disrupting old knowledge, schema-inconsistent ones were slower and "necessitated interleaved training with the already-known examples"; rat lesion 48 h after learning spares memory, plasticity genes up at 80 min; original timescale years-decades, now "a few hours to a couple of days" for consistent items.
- Numbers: the only one in [K16] is DQN with replay disabled falling "to at best 30%" of performance with replay (ref 119). [M95] simulation (Rumelhart net, same learning rate): focused learning of new facts is faster but raises error on the 15 old 'can' cases; interleaving avoids it; no percentage in text. [M95]: "If the rate of decay [of hippocampal traces] is relatively rapid in comparison with the rate of consolidation, much of the idiosyncratic content ... may not be consolidated at all".

## (e) ML precedents
- (i) MLP Memory [MLPM]: an MLP on pretrained-LM hidden states (~70% depth) is trained to output the kNN-LM distribution (k = 1024 over the training corpus, query excluded from its own neighbours), loss 0.4 KL + 0.6 CE; at inference it is interpolated with the LM, no datastore. WikiText-103 PPL, GPT2-large 15.81 -> 11.41 (221M MLP), 11.35 (359M), 11.25 (772M) [Table 13; baseline training unverified]. Mistral-7B QA average 32.12 -> 36.06 (+12.3%) vs kNN-LM 32.57 (+1.4%, fixed lambda 0.1) vs RAG 33.38 [Table 1]. "17.5%/24.1%" are scaling-exponent gains, not PPL cuts [Fig 5]. Cost: "equivalent to ... training a 7B parameter model for 1 epoch", 32 A800, 5 epochs [Sec 4, Table 4]. Caveat: a separate module kept at inference; the base LM is not improved.
- (ii) RETRO [RETRO Table 14]: trained with retrieval, evaluated with it off: C4 bpb baseline = OFF = 0.98/0.92/0.84/0.78 vs ON 0.82/0.77/0.71/0.66 (172M/425M/1.5B/7.5B), so the whole gain is lost; Wikitext103 PPL at 7.5B 10.65 baseline, 10.40 OFF, 2.22 ON (leakage-inflated). Retro-fitting: freeze weights, train only cross-attention and neighbour encoder (<10% of 7B weights) on 6M sequences = 3% of pre-training; OFF performance "exactly maintained" [Sec 4.2]. No fine-tune-to-drop-the-memory result found; Memorizing Transformers not opened (search snippet only, unverified).
- (iii) None found. Nearest hit, "Progressive distillation induces an implicit curriculum" (n-gram statistics as a progress measure, PCFG masked LM), is not an external-count scaffold; snippet only, unverified. Unread pointer from the searches: "Selective consolidation of learning and memory via recall-gated plasticity" (PMC11257680), a theory where recall gates consolidation.

## Inference (mine)
- RETRO OFF = baseline says co-training with a memory leaves no weight gain by default (the net offloads onto it, unharmed), so consolidation needs explicit pressure: distil the table's output as a target (MLP Memory is the one worked example, KL + CE, leave-one-out) or shrink or drop the table in training; no source tests the second.
- Fly and CLS agree on the shape: the slow store is written in parallel from the start (α/β needed from 15 min), the fast store matters for a window (fly 1.5-9 h; CLS hours-days for consistent items), consolidation interleaves old data at a small learning rate, and the trigger is spaced recurrence plus resources, a switch not a gain (P17); so schedule when to consolidate, and test transfer by removal rather than assuming it.
- If the table shrinks faster than the weights absorb, idiosyncratic entries are lost first (M95 race); confident, schema-consistent entries should consolidate fastest and with least interference (K16).
